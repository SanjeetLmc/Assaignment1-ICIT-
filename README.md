Past of Computers


The history of computers existed long before the electronic computers we see today. Instead, the history of computing began long before the world's first electronic computers that we use today. Long ago, various manmade mechanisms were used to ease calculations. These were generally light-weight devices that aided calculations. One of the earliest known counting devices included the abacus, widely used all over the world in the ancient period to perform addition, subtraction, and other basic calculations. Mechanical calculators using gear motions were invented in the 17th century.
The greatest name from the first period is Charles Babbage, an English mathematician. In the 1800s Babbage designed a machine called Analytical Engine. It was designed to take instructions, store a number and perform various calculations. Babbage did not quite complete his machine because the technology of that time was not able enough, but his work was very similar to even the most modern computers that exist nowadays. Because of this he is sometimes called the father of the computer.
The initial wave of electronic calculators emerged in the 1940s. They relied on vacuum tubes and were huge in size. Some enclosed an entire room and required huge amounts of electricity. As a result, they had a short shelf life and a tendency to break down. ENIAC is perhaps the best known of these early computers. But, using machines of this sort to carry out calculations was many times quicker than using a human. In subsequent years the development of the transistor and then the microchip made computers ever more portable, accessible, and affordable. By the 1970s and 80s a new generation of personal computers had emerged, which could be used on a rather more personal basis.

Life will become more comfortable and easier, but people will have to watch out for privacy problems and not become dependent on machinery.
